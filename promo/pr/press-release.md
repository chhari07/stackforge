## StackForge Labs opens testing of Stack, a free Android app that helps students remember what they read

*The open-source app turns highlights in articles, PDFs and books into notes, then brings them back each morning*

**[City], India, [date] 2026.** StackForge Labs today opened public testing of **Stack**, a free Android app built around one idea: *Stack makes you remember what you read.*

Students and self-learners read for hours every day, across news, coaching notes, PDFs and books, but most of it is forgotten within a week. Read-later apps help people save things, and note apps help them write things down, but few help them keep what they've read. Since Pocket shut down in July 2025, many readers have also been left looking for a new home for their saved articles.

Stack works in three steps:

- **Save:** share links, PDFs and text into Stack from any app, including Chrome, WhatsApp, Telegram and YouTube.
- **Highlight:** select a line in an article, PDF or book and it becomes a note that remembers its source.
- **Remember:** every morning, Stack brings back three old highlights on a spaced schedule, so the important lines stick.

The app also includes a focus timer with music, read-aloud, Indian news sources, a home-screen widget, and a full Hindi interface. Optional AI features can summarise an article or explain a difficult line in English or Hindi; the app always asks before sending any text.

"Every app helps you save things or write things down. None of them helped me *remember*," said **Aman Kumar Chhari, founder of StackForge Labs**. "I wanted the important lines to come back to me without making flashcards, so I built Stack."

Stack is free, has no ads, and works fully offline without an account. Its source code is public on GitHub.
